ADHD: information with care

You are not alone. This space is here to inform with clarity and offer support. If anything here resonates, take a calm breath — support and care are possible.

What is ADHD?

ADHD is a neurodevelopmental condition that can affect attention, impulsivity, and organization. Everyone experiences it differently, with strengths and challenges. With the right support, quality of life and wellbeing can improve.

How is it diagnosed?

Diagnosis is clinical and performed by qualified health professionals. It includes a detailed interview, life history, and evaluation of symptoms across contexts, sometimes with standardized questionnaires. There is no single test that “proves” ADHD.

Treatment and support

Care may include psychoeducation, psychotherapy, organization strategies, environmental adjustments, and in some cases, medication. The plan should be individualized and respectful of your needs.

Who can help?

Psychiatrists and psychologists are core professionals. In many cases, a multidisciplinary team also helps (e.g., neurology, occupational therapy, speech therapy). You deserve respectful, ongoing care.

Depression: understanding and support

Depression can make everyday life feel heavier. You deserve compassion, clarity, and a path to care.

What is depression?

Depression is more than sadness. It can affect mood, sleep, energy, and motivation. It’s a health condition, not a personal failure.

How is it diagnosed?

Diagnosis is clinical and based on symptoms, duration, and impact on life. A qualified professional will consider your history and current context.

Treatment and support

Care often includes therapy, lifestyle support, and sometimes medication. The best plan is tailored to you.

Who can help?

Psychiatrists, psychologists, and primary care clinicians can support you. Community services may also offer groups and guidance.

Anxiety: finding calm and safety

Anxiety can feel overwhelming, but you are not alone. Support exists, and relief is possible.

What is anxiety?

Anxiety involves persistent worry or fear that can affect daily life. It may show up as restlessness, racing thoughts, or physical tension.

How is it diagnosed?

A professional assesses symptoms, triggers, and impact. They may use structured interviews or questionnaires to guide care.

Treatment and support

Therapy, coping skills, and lifestyle adjustments are common. Medication can help some people. The plan should fit your needs.

Who can help?

Psychologists and psychiatrists are key. You can also seek support from primary care and trusted community services.

General wellbeing: gentle, everyday care

Small, consistent steps can support wellbeing. You don’t have to do it all at once.

Healthy routines

Sleep, movement, nutrition, and hydration can shape mood. Tiny changes are meaningful over time.

Stress and burnout

Stress is common, but sustained overload can lead to burnout. Building rest and boundaries helps protect your energy.

Connection matters

Supportive relationships help regulate emotions. You can start with one trusted person or community space.

When to seek help

If life feels unmanageable or you’re struggling daily, reaching out to a professional is a strong and healthy step.

You matter

If you’re feeling overwhelmed or experiencing depressive thoughts, please remember: your life matters. You are deeply loved by your family and by God. You are not alone, and support is available.

In Ireland, there are organisations that can help with depression, anxiety, and any mental health concern. If you are in immediate danger, call 112 or 999.

  • Samaritans Ireland
  • Pieta (Ireland)
  • Aware (Ireland)
  • Mental Health Ireland
  • Mental Health Europe
